IDUKKI

Idukki, located in the Western Ghats of Kerala, is known for its rich tribal heritage and diverse cultural mix. Unlike the coastal districts, Idukki’s culture is influenced by indigenous communities as well as settlers from other parts of Kerala, creating a blend of traditions, languages, and lifestyles. Agriculture plays a major role in daily life, with spice plantations like cardamom, pepper, and tea shaping both the economy and culture. Festivals from different religions are celebrated peacefully, reflecting harmony among Hindu, Christian, and Muslim communities. Geographically, Idukki is one of the most mountainous and forest-covered districts in Kerala, featuring dense forests, wildlife sanctuaries, and high-altitude hill ranges. It is home to the famous Idukki Dam, one of the highest arch dams in Asia, symbolizing the district’s importance in hydroelectric power. The environment is cool and misty compared to other parts of Kerala, with heavy rainfall supporting rich biodiversity. Rivers, valleys, and waterfalls add to its scenic beauty, making Idukki a major destination for nature and eco-tourism.
