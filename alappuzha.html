ALAPPUZHA

Alappuzha, often called the “Venice of the East,” is famous for its unique culture shaped by waterways and coastal life in Kerala. The district’s traditions are closely linked to its backwater lifestyle, with activities like snake boat races, especially during the festival of Nehru Trophy Boat Race, playing a major role in its cultural identity. People from Hindu, Muslim, and Christian communities live together, contributing to a rich mix of festivals, cuisine, and art forms, while coir-making and fishing are important traditional occupations. Geographically, Alappuzha is characterized by an extensive network of backwaters, lagoons, and canals connected to the Arabian Sea, making it one of the most water-dominated regions in India. The land is mostly low-lying and covered with paddy fields, coconut trees, and wetlands, creating a lush and scenic environment. The tropical climate with heavy monsoon rains supports agriculture and aquatic life, while the region’s biodiversity and natural beauty make it a major tourist destination known for houseboats and serene landscapes.
